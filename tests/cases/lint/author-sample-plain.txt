I wrote the first draft on a train, badly as it turned out, and kept it anyway. The second draft was shorter. My editor, who reads everything twice, cut the opening and left the ending alone. That was the right call. I have kept her notes in a folder for six years now, and I still read them before I start anything long. The habit costs ten minutes and saves a week. Most of what I know about structure came from those notes, not from books. When a piece goes wrong it is nearly always the opening, which tries to do too much, or the ending, which explains what the reader has already understood. I fix the opening first.
