# Release notes: 1.1.0 (18 November 2026)

This release rebuilds the humanizer as a general tool for removing the signs of AI writing. Two things drove it: the current revision of Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" field guide, and a test of this linter against a set of short examples of the structural patterns that current models produce. The rules on invented facts, verbatim citations and text-only delivery did not change.

## What the linter missed, and now catches

Before this release the linter was run on 26 short examples of AI writing patterns. It flagged 10. The misses were nearly all structural: pause-for-effect closers ("Let that sink in."), staged candour ("Honestly?"), rebuttals of objections nobody raised, the "This does not mean X. It means Y." contrast, the clipped tail (", no guessing."), prestige lists of outlets, vague association, and text that describes itself. This release flags 24 of the 26. The two left out on purpose are a hyphen after a noun and curly quotation marks, both weak signs that the humanizer leaves to the author's habit.

To check the new rules against human writing, the linter was run on about 74,000 words of Wikipedia article text. The new checks produced no hard findings and nine soft ones, mostly ordinary uses of "associated with". One rule ("while retaining the ...") was narrowed to the wording of machine edit notes after this run.

## What the Wikipedia guide changed

- Vocabulary is now described by model era, including the words that rose from mid-2026 (quietly, echoed, dependable, universally and others), five of which are soft context words in the linter.
- Corrective framing ("should be read as X rather than Y", "does not establish") and the emphasis on notability, coverage and online presence are new patterns.
- The catalogue lists signs that do not work (perfect grammar, mixed registers, formal prose, a lone transition word, missing citations) and signs that are now historical: promotional language, knowledge-cutoff statements, section summaries, elegant variation, and em dashes. Newer models avoid dashes and lean on full stops and commas instead, so the humanizer keeps its no-dash default but now tells the model to vary the punctuation it uses in their place.
- Human writing uses plain verbs, single qualifiers and small wordy constructions more than machine writing does, so filler is now cut only where it pads.

## Compatibility

No script option, skill or variant was removed or renamed. Drafts that passed 1.0.0 may now get new soft findings; new hard findings are limited to phrases a careful writer almost never uses and to chatbot artefacts. Form skills keep their "Departures from the humanizer".
